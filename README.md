# Vantage

Vantage helps sports fans better understand betting and prediction-market prices.

Sports markets move quickly, and it can be hard to tell whether a price is fair without checking multiple sources. Vantage puts these sources in one place, helping users see the current price, what the wider market suggests, and whether there may be an [edge](#what-is-an-edge).

Vantage is for learning and market analysis. It does not place trades, promise profit, or guarantee outcomes.

## Betting basics

### Odds

A price that reflects how likely an outcome is believed to be. Odds also determine a potential payout.

### American odds

A common odds format in the United States.

- `+150` means a successful $100 stake would earn $150 in profit.
- `-120` means a $120 stake would earn $100 in profit.

### Player prop

A market about a specific player statistic, such as points, assists, or total bases.

### Over / Under

A choice about whether a player or team will finish above or below a stated number. For example, “Over 24.5 points.”

### Market price

The current price available for an outcome or contract.

### Implied probability

The likelihood suggested by a market price or set of odds.

### Expected value (EV)

A way to estimate whether the price offered may be better or worse than the broader market's estimate. EV is an estimate, not a prediction.

### Closing line value (CLV)

The difference between the odds or price someone took and the market's final price when that market closes. In simple terms, it asks: **did you get a better price before the market moved?**

Positive CLV does not mean a position will win, and a losing position can still have positive CLV. It is a way to review the quality of a price, not a guarantee of an outcome.

### Price advantage

The difference between the current available price and Vantage's estimated market value.

### Confidence

A simple indicator of how current and complete the supporting market information is.

## What is an edge?

An **edge** simply means you may be getting a better price than the price the wider market suggests.

Think of it like shopping: one store sells something for $42, while most other stores say it is worth about $49. The $42 price could be a better deal. In Vantage, we compare the current contract price with wider market information to look for situations like that.

For example: if a contract costs 42 cents and the wider market suggests it may be worth closer to 49 cents, Vantage may show an estimated edge.

An edge does **not** mean the outcome will happen. It only means the current price may be better than expected. Markets can change, information can be incomplete, and every position carries risk.
